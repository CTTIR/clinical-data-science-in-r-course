## Preparation

- Print short case A (lesson page) on a separate sheet without the questions of W8; collect answers anonymously and
  keep them for the comparison with short case B in unit 32.
- Check that every laptop can run `Rscript r/w1-case-table.R`. A partner computer per three participants is enough.

## Typical mistakes

- Counting rows as patients (12 instead of 5).
- Fixing "P3" by deleting the row instead of correcting the identifier.
- Treating the free text as harmless because it contains no name: an occupation, a care-home room or a relative's
  ward can identify a person in a small hospital.
- Calling the learnt threshold "better" because it was learnt from data; with 40 people it moves from 1.3 cm to
  1.6 cm when other people are drawn.

## Answers to the lab questions

- **Part A counts:** naive 12 rows, 6 person identifiers, 7 images; corrected 12 readings, 7 images, 5 people. The
  unit error is in row 7 (174 instead of 17.4 cm).
- **Counter-check "7 of 12":** 7 of 12 readings are "fracture". At image level 4 of 7 images are read as fracture by
  every reader, 2 by none, and image P02-A is discordant (4 or 5 of 7, depending on the rule for discordance). At
  person level P01 and P04 have a fracture read, P03 and P05 not, P02 is discordant (2 or 3 of 5). The rule for
  discordant readings must be fixed before counting, ideally by a reference standard (for example follow-up
  imaging).
- **Part B:** the export row number and the free text are not needed; exact age is replaced by bands in Part D.
- **Part C:** rule (1.0 cm) gives 7 alerts and detects 5 of 5 fractures in the 20 application people, with
  2 false alerts; the learnt threshold (1.3 cm) gives 6 alerts and detects 4 of 5, with 2 false alerts.
  Counter-check: the threshold learnt from another 40 people is 1.6 cm.
- **Part D:** teaching table with 12 rows, pseudonyms, readings, wrist circumference, hours since injury and age
  band; no free text, no exact age.

## Three question types (model answers)

- Descriptive: proportion of adults with wrist pain after a fall who have a fracture on imaging (denominator: all
  such adults presenting in a defined period).
- Prognostic: who still has reduced grip strength after six weeks (predictors known at presentation).
- Causal: does an immediate second read reduce missed fractures (needs comparable groups with and without the
  second read).
