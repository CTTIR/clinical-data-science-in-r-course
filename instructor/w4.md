## Preparation

- Render the lab once before the session and print the six figures for groups working on paper.
- Prepare a blank split table on the board: aim, split, shared animal identifiers, correct/total spectra,
  correct/total animals, independent denominator.
- For unit 16, prepare two prompts: "Which features of a walking person in a video could identify them even without
  a face?" and "When could a combination of tissue features in a small cohort point to one person?"

## Typical mistakes

- Treating spectra (or pixels, or steps, or cells) as independent observations and reporting them as the sample
  size. The independent unit in Part B is the animal, in the gait station the person, in the tissue station the
  person.
- Reading 4/4 in the image split as evidence that the method "works". The lookup knows nothing about tissue; the
  result shows that the test design rewards recognising animals.
- Assuming that a ratio index is robust to any illumination change. It cancels a uniform change in brightness, not
  a change in the spectral composition of the light.
- Interpreting the Bland–Altman limits for per-person averages as limits for single steps.
- Reading the tissue result "positive cells lie closer to vessels" as biology without noticing that the threshold
  itself moves the result.

## Answers to the lab questions

**Part A.** Class means of the index with the original lamp: A 0.567, B 0.449 (difference 0.117). The weaker lamp
changes no index value (maximum absolute difference below 1e-12, checked by `stopifnot`). The tilted lamp raises
the index by 0.108 on average, so class B under the tilted lamp (about 0.57) resembles class A under the original
lamp: illumination confounds the class comparison if lamps differ between groups. White/dark reference correction
restores the original values (difference below 1e-10). Counter-check answer: no change for the weaker lamp; B looks
more like A under the tilted lamp.

**Part B (target values).** Image split: 4/4 test spectra correct, 4 shared animal identifiers. Animal split: empty
intersection of identifiers, 3/6 spectra and 1/2 animals correct; independent denominator 2 animals.
Counter-check with default class B: again 3/6 spectra and 1/2 animals, but now AN4 instead of AN2 is correct.
Extension: spectrum-wise 10-fold cross-validation 117/120 spectra correct, all 10 folds share animals;
leave-one-animal-out 64/120 spectra, majority correct for 6/12 animals, no shared identifiers in any fold. A good
answer states the limit: new animals of the same fictitious species and imaging set-up, not patients.

**Gait station.** Measured knee flexion with a side view 40.0 degrees, at 30 degrees camera rotation 35.0 degrees,
at 45 degrees 28.9 degrees. Per-person Bland–Altman (n = 20): bias 1.56 degrees, SD 1.90, limits of agreement −2.16
to 5.28 degrees; the upper limit exceeds the tolerance of ±5 degrees (assumption). 95% CI of the bias: 0.68 to 2.45
(persons, n = 20) versus 0.99 to 2.14 (steps, n = 100, falsely precise). A good consequence statement: a
systematic over-reading of about 1.6 degrees with occasional differences above 5 degrees could move a patient
across a rehabilitation threshold; a fixed camera position belongs in the protocol.

**Tissue station.** At threshold 0.5, 36% to 50% of cells per person are marker-positive (median 45%); at 0.6,
23% to 37% (median 30%). The median distance of positive cells to the nearest vessel falls in all six persons
(from about 30–42 µm to about 27–37 µm). Aggregation loss: the per-person median hides the spatial arrangement
within a section and the number of cells behind each value.

## Paper fallback

All parts can be completed from the printed output and the six figures. For Part B, hand out the four-animal table
(12 rows) and let teams apply the lookup rule with a pencil; the split table is the record. Alternative texts for
all figures are on the lab page.
