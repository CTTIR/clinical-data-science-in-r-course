## Preparation

- Plan W5 as a clinical–methodological tandem: one person with experience in spinal cord injury care or
  rehabilitation research, one with experience in longitudinal and causal methods. The clinician keeps the case
  realistic (why are samples missed, who is admitted to ICU), the methodologist keeps the estimand explicit.
- Render the lab once before the session and print Parts A and B for participants without a working R installation.
  The script runs in a fresh session in under 15 seconds (`Rscript r/w5-causality-trajectories.R`); figures and
  tables go to `output/`.
- Required packages: ggplot2, dagitty, lme4, splines (base R), cluster, mclust.
- Remind participants that the biomarker in Part A is generic; Part B names selenium only to give the unit a
  concrete meaning. Neither part makes a claim about real selenium biology.

## Typical mistakes

- Treating "ICU patients only" as a neutral restriction of the study population. It is conditioning on a common
  effect of the exposure and of a cause of the outcome.
- Adjusting for every available variable "to be safe". In this graph the correct adjustment set for the effect of
  the biomarker in the whole population is empty; after selecting on ICU admission, severity must be added.
- Counting 188 observations as the sample size. The independent unit is the person (n = 40).
- Reporting the completers' day-28 mean as the group mean.
- Reading the mixed model as a remedy for informative dropout. It uses the earlier measurements of those who drop
  out (valid under missing at random), but it cannot detect or correct missing not at random; that requires a
  sensitivity analysis.
- Taking a stable cluster solution as evidence of distinct subtypes, and forgetting that raw-scale k-means is
  dominated by the variable with the largest numerical spread.
- Choosing the spline because it "looks better" without a pre-specified comparison, or over-interpreting the
  completers' day-28 point that lies above both curves.

## Answers to the lab questions

All values are produced by the script with fixed seeds and checked by `stopifnot()`.

**Part A (unit 17).** `adjustmentSets()` returns `{}`; biomarker and outcome are d-separated unconditionally
(TRUE), not d-separated given ICU admission (FALSE), and d-separated again given ICU admission and severity (TRUE).
Change in motor score per 10 µg/L: all 5,000 patients +0.03 points; 1,588 ICU patients −1.05 points; ICU patients
adjusted for severity −0.00 points. Target checks: |all| < 0.3, ICU-only < −0.8, |adjusted| < 0.3.
Counter-check (ICU admission depends on severity only): +0.04 points per 10 µg/L, i.e. the association vanishes.
Model answer for the target sentence: "Causal estimand: the difference in mean six-month motor score if the
biomarker were lowered by 10 µg/L in all patients admitted with spinal cord injury. Severity is a cause of the
outcome but not of the biomarker in our assumed graph, so it is not needed in the whole population; ICU admission
is a collider and must not be used for selection or adjustment. If we only have ICU patients, we must adjust for
severity." A prognostic target ("predict the motor score of ICU patients from admission data") may legitimately use
the biomarker as a predictor.

**Part B (unit 18).** 40 persons, 188 observations, 12 persons without a day-28 sample (9 complete, 3 incomplete
lesion). Per-person slopes: mean 2.26 (complete) and 4.17 µg/L per week (incomplete), difference 1.91 (Welch
*t*-test *p* = 0.005). Mixed model: interaction 2.05 µg/L per week (Wald 95% CI 0.93 to 3.17). Day-28 means
(without dropout / completers / mixed model with one mean per day): complete 68.2 / 72.4 / 69.2, incomplete
76.4 / 78.1 / 77.1 µg/L. Sensitivity (δ = 0, 5, 10 µg/L): day-28 mean complete 69.2, 67.0, 64.7; incomplete 77.1,
76.3, 75.6; interaction 2.29, 2.67, 3.05 µg/L per week.
Counter-check answer: as δ grows the difference between the groups grows, because 9 of the 12 missing samples
belong to the complete-lesion group, which therefore loses more. The δ = 0 interaction (2.29) differs from the
observed-data fit (2.05) because the filled values come from the model with one mean per day, which follows the
levelling-off of the curve.
Background for the instructor only: the data were generated with an initial rise of 5 (complete) and 8 µg/L per week
(incomplete) and a common curvature of −0.8 µg/L per week²; dropout probability rises as the unobserved day-28
value falls. This is known only because the data are synthetic and should not be presented as the "true answer" to
participants, who never have it in a real study.

**Station 1 (unit 19).** Standard deviations of the raw markers: 9.3, 26.1, 0.5 and 117.3. Adjusted Rand index
between raw and standardised solutions 0.05; rank correlation of standardised clusters with the gradient 0.90,
of raw clusters 0.08; median bootstrap adjusted Rand index 0.66; mean silhouette width 0.30; cluster sizes 98,
103 and 99. Counter-check: without the unrelated marker the rank correlation rises to 0.92. Expected conclusion:
the "subtypes" are cuts of one continuous gradient; their number and borders follow from k and the scaling. A
subtype hypothesis would need a pre-specified external check, for example different outcomes in an independent
cohort.

**Station 2 (unit 19).** AIC 1138.0 (linear, 8 parameters) against 1126.7 (natural spline with 3 degrees of
freedom, 12 parameters). Expected conclusion: the rise levels off within four weeks; a slope per week is a rough
summary. The day-28 point of the complete-lesion group lies above both curves because it averages completers only.

**Unit 20 (model answer outline).** Hypothesis: "In the rodent model, the biomarker rise in the first four weeks is
larger after incomplete than after complete lesions." Replacement: re-analyse existing human registry data and
published animal data before any new experiment; in-vitro models cannot reproduce a whole-body trajectory, so
partial replacement only. Reduction: the animal is the unit; use a repeated-measures design with all time points
per animal, a pre-specified effect size and a sample size calculation on the animal level; share controls with
ongoing studies if permitted. Refinement: minimally invasive sampling volumes and frequencies, analgesia, humane
end points. Species and transfer: state which feature of the human injury the model reproduces and which question
stays *open* for people. Criterion against a further experiment: if registry data already show the difference with
adequate precision, or if no plausible clinical consequence follows from either result. People affected: patients
with spinal cord injury and their teams would only benefit if a later human study tests a decision that depends
on the biomarker. Use the NC3Rs page (https://nc3rs.org.uk/who-we-are/3rs) and ARRIVE 2.0
(https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000410) as references.

## Paper fallback

Hand out the rendered Part A and Part B output (tables and figures) without code. Participants draw the DAG,
mark the collider, read the three slopes off the table, count persons and observations from the spaghetti plot
caption and write the report sentence. Station 1 works with the printed cluster figure and the four standard
deviations; station 2 with the AIC table and the curve figure.

## Open points

  review by the clinical member of the tandem before the first run. Responsible role: course lead.
- Whether a real, published longitudinal biomarker data set with a clear licence could replace Part B in a later
  edition: *open*. Responsible role: editorial team.
- Local rules for any animal experiment discussed in unit 20 (animal welfare body, licensing authority) are not
  part of the course and remain *open* for each institution. Responsible role: the participants' research group.
