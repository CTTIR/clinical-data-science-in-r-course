# Instructor notes · W6 · Pharmacometrics, forecasting and simulation

This folder is excluded from the rendered course website but is public in the source repository. It contains no
assessment items; the item pool for the individual examination is kept outside this repository.

Lab page: `exercises/lab-w6-forecast-pk-simulation.qmd` · script: `r/w6-forecast-pk-simulation.R` (fresh session:
`Rscript r/w6-forecast-pk-simulation.R`, about 3 seconds) · data written by the lab: `data/w6-ed-arrivals.csv`,
`data/w6-pk-concentrations.csv`, `data/w6-mci-events.csv`. All data are synthetic; the pharmacokinetic parameters

## Preparation

- Plan a clinical–methodological tandem: one person from emergency medicine, anaesthesia or surgery (staffing,
  theatre organisation, plausibility of the scenarios) and one person from statistics or pharmacometrics
  (rolling-origin evaluation, ODE solvers, priors).
- Render the lab page and print the five figures and the result tables for groups without a working R installation.
- Check that `deSolve` and `simmer` load on the room computers (`library(deSolve); library(simmer)`).

## Typical mistakes

- Part A: evaluating forecasts on randomly chosen hours, or letting the 4-week mean include weeks after the target
  hour. The `stopifnot()` line on `history_end < target_index` is the check to point at.
- Part A: treating the 80% interval as a guarantee. It is learnt from past errors and loses coverage when the level
  changes.
- Part A: judging the rules by MAE alone. The 4-week mean has the lower MAE even during the wave, but it is biased
  downwards in exactly the busiest shift.
- Station PK: changing the solver tolerance after seeing the result, or comparing at different time points. Units
  and tolerance are fixed before computing.
- Station PK: reading half-life as a property of clearance alone. It depends on the ratio V/CL.
- Station operating capacity: running each scenario with its own random numbers and attributing the whole
  difference to capacity. Common random numbers isolate the capacity effect.
- Station operating capacity: reporting only the mean waiting time, without the spread over replications.
- Part C: reading the posterior as a treatment effect. There is no comparison group.

## Answers to the lab questions

Values from the script with the seeds in the file; they are checked with `stopifnot()` where marked (✓).

**Part A (unit 21)**

- Data: 2,688 hours (16 weeks) ✓; daily totals 80 to 190 arrivals, mean 126.1.
- Every forecast uses only hours before its origin ✓; 2 rules × 8 forecast weeks × 168 hours = 2,688 forecast
  hours ✓, 672 per rule and period ✓.
- Before the wave (weeks 9–12): MAE 1.83 (4-week mean) vs 2.26 (same hour last week) arrivals per hour ✓; mean error
  0.07 vs 0.04; 80% coverage 81.0% vs 87.8%.
- During the wave (weeks 13–16): MAE 2.18 vs 2.63; mean error +0.81 vs +0.33 (both under-forecast ✓); coverage 78.3%
  vs 82.4%.
- Under-forecast per 8-hour shift during the wave, 4-week mean: night 4.76, early 6.45, late 8.15 arrivals (largest
  in the late shift ✓); same hour last week: 2.71, 1.61, 3.57.
- Horizon: MAE barely changes from 1–24 h to 73–168 h (4-week mean 2.10, 2.05, 1.96; last week 2.55, 2.49, 2.40).
- Counter-check (mean error by forecast week): week 13: 1.33 vs 1.25; week 14: 1.07 vs 0.02; week 15: 0.38 vs −0.33;
  week 16: 0.45 vs 0.37. From week 14 on, last week's value already contains the wave ✓; the 4-week mean lags until
  all four weeks lie inside the wave.
- A good handover sentence names the late shift, says that the interval assumes an unchanged process and proposes a
  trigger for manual review after a sudden rise.

**Part B, station PK (unit 22)**

- Maximal relative difference ODE vs analytic: 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ < 10⁻⁶ ✓.
- C(0) = 2 mg/L ✓, half-life ln 2 · 50 / 5 = 6.93 h ✓, AUC(0–∞) = 100/5 = 20 mg·h/L ✓.
- One parameter at a time: CL 2.5 L/h → half-life 13.86 h, C(12 h) 1.10 mg/L, AUC 40; CL 10 L/h → 3.47 h, 0.18 mg/L,
  AUC 10 (C(0) unchanged at 2 mg/L). V 25 L → C(0) 4 mg/L, half-life 3.47 h, AUC 20; V 100 L → C(0) 1 mg/L, 13.86 h,
  AUC 20. All variations agree with the analytic solution within tolerance ✓.
- Counter-check (CL 10 L/h and V 100 L): half-life unchanged at 6.93 h ✓, C(0) halved to 1 mg/L ✓. Two late samples
  show the same slope, so only an early sample (or the known amount and an early concentration) separates CL from V.

**Part B, station operating capacity (unit 22)**

- 200 replications × 12 casualties; base R and simmer give identical waiting times for replication 1 (difference
  < 10⁻⁸ min) ✓; with three theatres no casualty waits longer than with two on the same sequence ✓.
- Mean waiting time per casualty (median, 10th–90th percentile over replications): 2.45 h (1.72–3.32) with two
  theatres, 1.08 h (0.57–1.68) with three; paired difference 1.37 h (1.07–1.64), never negative ✓.
- Share waiting more than 2 h: 0.58 (0.42–0.67) vs 0.17 (0.00–0.42).
- Counter-check (operations 20% longer, three theatres): median 1.52 h, between the original three-theatre value
  (1.08 h) and the two-theatre value (2.45 h) ✓.

**Part C (unit 23)**

- Posterior Beta(4, 10) with the flat prior and Beta(5, 17) with the sceptical prior ✓; posterior means 28.6% and
  22.7%.
- 95% equal-tailed intervals: 9.1% to 53.8% and 8.2% to 41.9%; the grid calculation agrees within 0.001 ✓.
- Counter-check (30 of 120): Beta(31, 91) → 18.1% to 33.5%; Beta(32, 98) → 17.6% to 32.3%. Both intervals are
  narrower ✓ and the prior matters much less.
- A good sentence states the observed 3/12, gives one interval with its prior and says explicitly that without a
  control group nothing can be concluded about a treatment effect. The 3Rs discussion should end with a criterion
  for or against another experiment (for example: only with a concurrent control group and a pre-specified question
  that this result cannot answer).

## Paper fallback

Groups without a working R installation receive the rendered lab page as a printout: the daily arrival figure, the
forecast figure for week 13 and both error tables (Part A), the PK figure and parameter table, the waiting-time
figure and summary table (Part B) and the prior/posterior figure with the interval table (Part C). They answer the
"predict first" questions on paper, then compare with the printed counter-check outputs. In unit 24 such a group
reviews another group's handover instead of running a script.

## Open points

- Whether hourly forecasts of this kind are used for staffing in a real department, and at what lead time, is
  *open*; to be clarified by the clinical member of the teaching tandem with the department's nursing management.
- Typical surgery durations and casualty numbers for a local mass casualty plan are *open*; the values here are
  teaching assumptions. To be checked by the course team with the hospital's emergency planning officer before
  adapting the scenario.
- The sceptical prior Beta(2, 8) stands for "earlier similar work"; a real prior needs a documented source. To be
  discussed with the methodological member of the tandem when groups transfer the example to their project.
