## Typical mistakes

- Using the total number of examinations, or the number of fractures, as the denominator of the PPV.
- Concluding from the lower PPV that the tool is "bad"; the PPV changes with prevalence even at identical
  sensitivity and specificity.
- Computing the median for imputation on all 2,000 admissions (leaks test information).
- Reading an AUC of 0.86 as "86% correct".
- Reporting subgroup sensitivity without the number of fractures behind it.
- In the journal club, reading accuracy results as evidence of patient benefit.

## Answers to the lab questions

- **Part A:** TP/FP/FN/TN = 45/95/5/855 and 180/80/20/720; PPV 45/140 = 32.1% and 180/260 = 69.2%; NPV 99.4% and
  97.3%. Counter-check: with specificity 95% at 5% prevalence, false alerts fall to 47.5 per 1,000 and the PPV
  rises to 48.6%.
- **Part B:** 1,400 training and 600 test admissions (84 fractures in the test set), zero shared identifiers;
  training median of grip strength 30.3 kg. Null model Brier score 0.121; baseline AUC 0.858, Brier 0.091,
  calibration slope 0.86; elastic net AUC 0.883, Brier 0.080, calibration slope 1.18. The gain in ranking (0.025) is
  small compared with the effort for tuning and documentation.
- **Part C:** threshold 10% / 20% / 30% / 40%: 240 / 163 / 124 / 92 alerts; sensitivity 84.5% / 71.4% / 64.3% /
  56.0%; false alerts per shift of 30 examinations 8.5 / 5.2 / 3.5 / 2.3. At 20%: age 18–74 detected 35/46 = 76.1%
  (61.2% to 87.4%), false alerts 60/385 = 15.6%; age 75 and older detected 25/38 = 65.8% (48.6% to 80.4%), false
  alerts 43/131 = 32.8% (24.9% to 41.6%). Older patients carry both more missed fractures and more unnecessary
  follow-up in this synthetic cohort (built in by design: weaker clinical signs in older patients).
- **Calibration:** the baseline overestimates risk in the highest fifth (predicted about 56%, observed about 45%).

## Journal club: model answer for Wong et al. (2021)

Recruitment: adults hospitalised in one academic health system, December 2018 to October 2019. Reference standard:
composite sepsis definition (surveillance criteria or diagnosis codes with organ dysfunction). Analysis:
hospitalisation-level AUC 0.63 (95% CI 0.62–0.64), calibration plots, alerting strategies. Generalisation: one
health system; the proprietary model was developed elsewhere. Decision: the study shows poor discrimination and
calibration in this setting and a large alert burden; it does not study patient outcomes with and without the
model. Next study: a prospective, comparative evaluation (for example cluster-randomised or stepped-wedge) with
patient-relevant outcomes, after local recalibration.
