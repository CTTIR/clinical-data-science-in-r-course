## Preparation

- Draw the perspectives (clinical use, patients, methods, regulation, data protection, information security) per
  presentation before unit 29 so that every project is questioned from at least four perspectives.
- Two assessors per presentation score independently with the rubric and agree afterwards; use the worked example
  on the rubric page to calibrate before the first presentation.
- Examination: take the papers from the private item pool; check the distribution against
  `assessment-blueprint.md`; arrange the accessibility adjustments that were requested.
- Prepare short case B on a separate sheet; keep the answers to short case A from unit 01 for comparison.

## Typical mistakes in the defences

- Presenting the model passport but no decision.
- A recommendation without a stop condition or review date.
- Answering a data protection question with "we pseudonymise".
- Reporting the number of images or steps as sample size.

## Evaluation: model answers for short cases A and B

Both cases have the same structure; score each part as correct, partly correct or incorrect.

- **(a) Independent number of units:** A: 20 rats (not 1,200 images). B: 15 patients (not 3,000 frames).
- **(b) What the figure measures:** in both cases, mainly recognition of individuals seen in training (images of
  the same rat, frames of the same patient in training and test); it does not show generalisation to new
  individuals.
- **(c) First two steps:** contain (stop further distribution, ask the recipient to delete and confirm, remove the
  post or message) and secure facts and inform the responsible roles (information security, data protection
  officer, project lead); the assessment of notification follows. In case A the spreadsheet contains staff names but
  no patient data; in case B a patient's face and ward are visible, so health-related personal data are involved.

Report the share of correct answers per part (denominator: participants who answered both cases). The comparison is
for course revision only; it is not a measure of individual performance and not evidence of a causal effect.
